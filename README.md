## Hi, I'm Ayana

I work on **natural language processing for low-resource, mixed-language text**. My main project is named entity recognition for public transport complaints written in Kazakh, Russian, and often both within one message, and understanding *why* such models fail on the entities that matter most.

- **Now:** analyzing train–test representation shift in a Kazakh–Russian NER model with optimal transport and permutation tests
- **Published:** first-authored paper at IEEE SIST 2026
- **Education:** B.Sc. in Computer Science, Astana IT University (2023–2026)
- **Languages:** Kazakh (native), Russian (fluent), English

**Research interests:** multilingual and low-resource NLP · Kazakh and code-switching · information extraction · representation analysis · optimal transport and distribution shift

### Research

**[kazru-ner-representation-shift](https://github.com/ayanaussenbayeva/kazru-ner-representation-shift)** · *2026, in progress*<br>
Why does a multilingual NER model fail on street names? A follow-up to my paper that measures the train–test shift in the encoder's representation space with the sliced Wasserstein distance and validates it with permutation and size-matched null tests. Main results: the shift is real, fine-tuning amplifies it about fivefold, and language mixing is *not* its cause, contrary to my initial hypothesis.<br>
<sub>PyTorch · XLM-RoBERTa · POT (optimal transport) · scikit-learn</sub>

**Entity extraction for geotagging public transport complaints** · *2025–2026, B.Sc. thesis (grade A−)*<br>
Fine-tuned XLM-RoBERTa to extract six entity types (route numbers, bus IDs, plate numbers, stop names, street names, intersections) from 2,750 manually annotated complaints in Kazakh, Russian and mixed text, to map complaints for spatio-temporal analysis. Developed within a national research grant (BR24992852); the basis of the publication below.<br>
<sub>PyTorch · Hugging Face Transformers · seqeval</sub>
